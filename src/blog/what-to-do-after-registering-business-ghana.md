After registering a business in Ghana, check your documents, arrange your tax affairs, find out which local and sector permits you need, separate the business's money, set up proper records and create a compliance calendar.

If you employ workers, you should also address payroll and SSNIT registration.

Your ORC certificate is an important beginning, but it does not automatically complete every requirement for operating the business.


## 1. Check and Safely Store Your Registration Documents

Read every document issued by the Office of the Registrar of Companies (ORC). Check the spelling of:

- The registered business or company name
- The proprietor's, directors' or shareholders' names
- The principal business address
- The stated business activities
- The registration number

Ask the ORC how to correct an error before using the documents for a bank account, licence, contract or website.

Keep the originals safely and create clear digital copies. 


## 2. Confirm Your Tax Registration and Obligations

Contact the Ghana Revenue Authority (GRA) or visit a Taxpayer Service Centre to confirm:

- The tax account connected to you or the entity
- Which taxes apply to the business
- Which returns you must file
- The filing and payment deadlines
- Whether you must register for VAT or another specific tax


The GRA lists filing returns on time, paying taxes on time, keeping proper business records and informing it about business changes among a taxpayer's obligations. See the [GRA's taxpayer rights and obligations](https://gra.gov.gh/domestic-tax/taxpayer-rights-and-obligations/).

## 3. Ask Your Local Assembly About a Business Operating Permit

Business registration and permission to operate from a particular location are not necessarily the same thing.

Contact the Metropolitan, Municipal or District Assembly responsible for your business location. Ask whether you need a Business Operating Permit, signage approval, environmental-health inspection or another local permit.

For example, the Accra Metropolitan Assembly lists an ORC business certificate or certificate of incorporation, the business name, owner's name, location and contact details in its [Business Operating Permit checklist](https://www.ama.gov.gh/bop.php).


## 4. Check for Industry-Specific Licences

Some activities need approval from a sector regulator in addition to ORC registration and a local operating permit.

This may apply to businesses dealing with areas such as food, health, education, financial services, tourism, transport, construction or regulated professional services.

Make a short description of exactly what the business will sell and how it will operate. Send it to the appropriate regulator and ask:

1. Whether a licence, registration or inspection is required
2. Which documents must be submitted
3. What must be completed before trading begins
4. Whether the approval must be renewed

A registration certificate should not be treated as proof that a regulated activity has been authorised.

## 5. Separate Business and Personal Money

Set up a suitable business bank account, merchant account or business mobile-money arrangement where available for your entity type.

Separation makes it easier to:

- Track sales and expenses
- Prepare accounts and tax returns
- Identify unpaid customer invoices
- Understand whether the business is profitable
- Produce records when a bank, investor or authority requests them

Before choosing a provider, compare its required documents, transaction charges, withdrawal limits, settlement time and account-security controls.

Avoid using one account for household spending, customer payments and supplier payments if you can reasonably separate them.

## 6. Create a Simple Bookkeeping System

You do not need to wait until the business becomes large. Start recording transactions from the first sale.

At minimum, capture:

| Record | Useful details |
| --- | --- |
| Sales | Date, customer, item or service, amount and payment method |
| Expenses | Date, supplier, purpose, amount and receipt |
| Customer invoices | Invoice number, due date, amount and payment status |
| Stock | Quantity received, quantity sold, damaged items and balance |
| Business assets | Item, purchase date, cost and location |

Number invoices consistently and back up records. Keep the source documents, not only a total written in a notebook.

The [GRA](https://gra.gov.gh/domestic-tax/taxpayer-rights-and-obligations/) specifically identifies keeping and maintaining proper business records as a taxpayer obligation.

## 7. Complete Employer and Payroll Setup Before Hiring

If the business employs workers, prepare written employment terms, payroll records and the applicable statutory registrations before problems arise.

SSNIT provides an employer-registration process and issues an Employer Registration number for future contribution payments. Review the current requirements on SSNIT's [Become an Employer page](https://www.ssnit.org.gh/become-an-employer/) or contact SSNIT directly.

Ask an accountant or employment professional to explain payroll deductions, PAYE, pension contributions and employment-record requirements for your situation. Do not treat money paid to a worker as an informal expense with no documentation.

If you work alone, SSNIT also provides routes for employees and self-employed people to join the scheme. Check the current options directly with SSNIT.

## 8. Protect Customer and Business Information

Even a small business may collect names, phone numbers, delivery locations, email addresses or payment-related information.

Limit access to this data. Use unique passwords, turn on two-factor authentication where available and remove access promptly when a worker or contractor leaves.

If you collect personal data through a website, form, loyalty programme or customer database, check your responsibilities with Ghana's [Data Protection Commission](https://dataprotection.org.gh/). Explain what information you collect, why you collect it and who can access it.

Never ask customers to send card PINs, mobile-money PINs or one-time verification codes.

## 9. Secure Your Business Name Online

Once the registered name is confirmed, check for a suitable domain name and matching social-media usernames.

A practical online setup may include:

- A domain name that is short and easy to spell
- A professional email address using the domain
- A website explaining what the business offers
- Accurate contact information and opening hours
- A Google Business Profile where appropriate
- Consistent branding across social platforms

Register important digital accounts in the business owner's name or an account the business controls. Record who owns the domain, where it is registered and when it expires.

## 10. Build a Compliance Calendar

Create one calendar for ORC, GRA, assembly, licence, insurance, domain and other recurring deadlines.

The type of ORC task depends on the entity. Business-name registrations are renewed annually, while incorporated companies file annual returns. These are different processes.

Set more than one reminder and assign responsibility for each task. After filing or paying, save the official acknowledgement instead of relying on a verbal promise from an agent.

## A Practical First-30-Days Checklist

### During the First Week

- Check the registration documents
- Create digital backups
- Confirm tax-registration steps with the GRA
- Contact the relevant local assembly
- Identify any sector regulator

### During the First Month

- Separate business and personal money
- Start bookkeeping
- Prepare invoices and receipts
- Complete employer setup if hiring
- Secure the domain and essential online accounts
- Add all recurring deadlines to a calendar

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can I Start Trading Immediately After ORC Registration?

That depends on the activity and location. Ask your local assembly and any relevant sector regulator whether a permit, inspection or licence is required before opening.

### Do I Need a Business Bank Account?

Ask banks what they offer for your registered structure. Even where a particular account is not a condition of ORC registration, keeping business money separate is useful for accounting, tax and cash-flow management.

### Is My ORC Certificate the Same as a Business Operating Permit?

No. ORC registration identifies the registered entity or business name. A Business Operating Permit is handled by the relevant local assembly.

### What Should I Do Before Building a Website?

Confirm the correct business name, secure the domain, prepare accurate contact details and decide what customers should be able to do on the site. This may include calling, requesting a quote, booking, paying or placing an order.
