To register a **.com.gh** domain, choose an available name, use a registrar that handles **.gh** domains, enter the correct registrant details, pay for the registration period and connect the domain to your website or email service.

The Ghana Domain Name Registry (GDNR) manages Ghana's domain-name space, including **.gh**, **.com.gh**, **.org.gh** and **.edu.gh**. The registry explains that **.com.gh** is intended for companies. See the [official GDNR website](https://gdnr.org.gh/).

*Registry information checked on 15 September 2026. Registrar prices, requirements and processing times can change, so confirm them before paying.*

## What Is a .com.gh Domain?

A **.com.gh** domain is a Ghana-focused website address such as **example.com.gh**. The name you choose appears before **.com.gh**.

The GDNR describes it as a third-level domain:

- **.gh** is Ghana's country-code domain.
- **com** indicates the commercial category.
- Your chosen name identifies your business.

A **.com.gh** address can make the Ghana connection clear to customers. However, it does not register your business, create a trademark or provide web hosting by itself.

## Step 1: Choose a Suitable Domain Name

Start with the approved business or brand name. Good domain names are usually:

- Short
- Easy to spell
- Easy to say over the phone
- Free from unnecessary hyphens and numbers
- Broad enough to remain useful if the business expands

Prepare two or three alternatives. A domain may already be registered even if the matching business name is available at the Office of the Registrar of Companies.

## Step 2: Check Whether the Name Is Available

Use the domain-search tool provided by a registrar that offers **.com.gh** registrations. Enter the complete proposed address, including **.com.gh**.

Check the spelling carefully before paying. A registrar cannot simply correct a registered domain as if it were a typing error. You may have to register the correct name separately.

Do not assume that availability gives you the right to use another person's business name or trademark. If the name could conflict with an existing brand, get appropriate legal advice before building around it.

## Step 3: Choose a .gh Registrar

A **registry** manages the domain extension, while a **registrar** accepts registration requests from customers and manages domains on their behalf.

GDNR says a registrar may be an internet service provider, web designer or solution provider that registers a domain for the actual owner, called the registrant. Start from the [GDNR website](https://gdnr.org.gh/) and confirm that the provider currently handles **.gh** domains.

Before choosing, ask the registrar:

1. What is the registration price for the first term?
2. What is the current renewal price?
3. How long is the registration period?
4. Which documents or verification details are required?
5. Will the domain be registered in my name or my company's name?
6. Can I manage DNS records myself?
7. What is the process and cost for transferring the domain?
8. What support is available if the domain stops working?

Do not compare only the introductory price. Renewal cost, ownership control and support are important too.

## How Much Does a .com.gh Domain Cost?

There is no single price that should be quoted for every provider. Registrars set their customer prices and may bundle the domain with hosting, email, SSL or website services.

Request a written quotation that separates:

- Domain registration
- Annual or multi-year renewal
- Web hosting
- Email hosting
- Website design
- Optional security or support services

Treat any price shown in an old article as an estimate until the registrar confirms it. The amount charged today may differ from the renewal amount in a later year.

## Step 4: Submit the Registrant Information

The registrant should be the person or organisation that is meant to control the domain.

Provide accurate:

- Registrant or company name
- Email address
- Phone number
- Postal or physical address where requested
- Business documents or verification information requested by the registrar

Use an email address you control and check regularly. Renewal notices, verification messages and transfer approvals may be sent there.

If a web developer registers the domain for you, ask for written confirmation that your business is recorded as the registrant. Also request access to the registrar account or a clear handover process.

## Step 5: Pay and Confirm the Registration

Check the complete domain name one last time before paying. Confirm the registration period and whether any hosting or email service has been added.

After payment:

1. Save the receipt.
2. Keep the registrar's confirmation email.
3. Confirm that the domain appears in your account.
4. Record the registration and expiry dates.
5. Store support and account-recovery details safely.

GDNR says domains are generally registered for an initial period of one to five years and must be renewed after that period. The domain remains assigned to you while it is registered and active. See the [GDNR frequently asked questions](https://gdnr.org.gh/).

## Step 6: Connect the Domain to Your Website

Registering the domain does not automatically create a website.

To display a website, the domain must point to a hosting service. This is usually done through **DNS records** or by changing the domain's **nameservers**.

Your web host or developer should provide the exact records. Copy them carefully. DNS changes may take time to appear everywhere, so avoid repeatedly changing settings while they are still updating.

Before replacing existing DNS settings, save a copy. Incorrect changes can affect both the website and business email.

The GDNR also explains that a domain needs additional services, such as web hosting, before it can display a website.

## Step 7: Create a Professional Email Address

Once the domain is working, you can create addresses such as:

- **hello@example.com.gh**
- **sales@example.com.gh**
- **accounts@example.com.gh**

Email hosting is separate from the domain unless it is included in your package. Ask the provider about mailbox storage, spam protection, backups and account recovery.

For better delivery, the email provider may ask you to add DNS records such as MX, SPF, DKIM or DMARC. Use the records supplied by that provider rather than copying settings from another domain.

## Protect the Domain After Registration

Your domain controls access to an important part of the business's online identity. Protect it by:

- Using a unique password for the registrar account
- Enabling two-factor authentication if available
- Keeping the registrant email and phone number current
- Restricting account access to trusted people
- Turning on renewal reminders
- Recording who is responsible for renewing it
- Keeping backup copies of important DNS records

Avoid registering a business domain through a former employee's personal account. If an agency manages it, include domain ownership and handover terms in the agreement.

## Should You Register .com.gh, .gh or .com?

The best option depends on the brand, audience, budget and availability.

| Extension | What it may communicate |
| --- | --- |
| **.com.gh** | A commercial organisation connected with Ghana |
| **.gh** | A short, direct Ghanaian identity |
| **.com** | A widely recognised international commercial address |

You may register more than one version to protect the brand, then redirect the alternatives to the main website. Only do this if the additional renewal costs fit your budget.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

### Letting Someone Else Own the Domain

Paying for the domain does not help if the registrant details and account remain under someone else's control. Confirm ownership from the beginning.

### Confusing a Domain with Hosting

The domain is the address. Hosting stores and serves the website. You usually need both for a working site.

### Forgetting the Renewal Date

An expired domain can interrupt the website and email. Use calendar reminders in addition to the registrar's notices.

### Buying Before Checking the Name

Check the business name, domain spelling and possible brand conflicts before printing signs, packaging or marketing materials.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can I Register a .com.gh Domain Without a Website?

Yes. You can register the domain first and connect a website later. Ask the registrar what default page or DNS settings will be used in the meantime.

### Does the Domain Include Web Hosting?

Not automatically. Some providers sell a bundle, while others charge separately. Check the quotation.

### Is a .com.gh Domain Mine Forever?

No. It is registered for a defined period and must be renewed. According to GDNR, the domain remains yours as long as it is registered and active.

### Can My Web Designer Register It for Me?

Yes, but your business should be identified as the registrant and should have a documented way to control or recover the account.

### Can I Move the Domain to Another Provider?

Ask the registrar about its transfer procedure, eligibility rules and charges before registering. Keep your registrant contact information accurate because transfer approval may depend on it.

## Final Checklist

Before you finish:

1. Confirm the spelling and availability of the domain.
2. Verify that the provider handles **.gh** domains.
3. Compare registration, renewal and transfer terms.
4. Use accurate registrant details.
5. Save the receipt and account access.
6. Connect the correct website and email DNS records.
7. Protect the account and schedule renewal reminders.
